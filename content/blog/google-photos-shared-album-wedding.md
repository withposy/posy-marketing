It's a few weeks before the wedding and the budget spreadsheet has started to look at you funny. Someone in the group chat says, "Why pay for a photo app? Just make a Google Photos album." And honestly, it's a fair question.

A Google Photos shared album is free, it works on almost any phone, and you probably have the app already. For plenty of weddings, it's all you need. For others, the small print turns into a very long week of chasing photos.

Here's how to set one up properly, step by step, plus where it falls short at a wedding and what works better.

## How to Set Up a Google Photos Shared Album for Your Wedding

The whole thing takes about ten minutes. Do it a few weeks before the wedding so you have time to test it.

### Step 1: Create the album

Open Google Photos on your phone, tap the **+** button and choose **Album**. Give it a name your guests will recognise instantly, like "Sophie & Tom's Wedding", rather than "Wedding album 2". Add a couple of photos yourselves, maybe from the engagement, so the album doesn't look empty when the first guest opens it.

### Step 2: Turn on sharing and create a link

In the album, tap **Share**, then **Create link**. Google is clear about what this means: anyone who has the link can view the album, and when you share a link, anyone with it can also add photos to the album.

### Step 3: Check that Collaborate is switched on

In the album's sharing options, make sure **Collaborate** is on. This is the setting that lets guests add their own photos and videos. If it's off, your guests can look but not contribute, which rather defeats the point.

### Step 4: Turn the link into a QR code

Google Photos can show a QR code for a shared album, and anyone who scans it can open the album. If you'd rather design your own sign, copy the link and paste it into our free [wedding photo QR code maker](/wedding-photo-qr-code/), which turns it into printable table cards and a welcome sign in your own colours.

### Step 5: Put it everywhere guests pause

Welcome sign, table cards, the bar, the back of the order of service. A small card on every table works better than one big sign by the door, because nobody scans a QR code on their way in. They do it twenty minutes into dinner, phone already in hand.

### Step 6: Test it on a friend's phone

This is the step everyone skips. Ask a friend, ideally one with an iPhone who doesn't use Google Photos, to scan the code and add a photo. You'll find out in two minutes exactly what your guests will see, including any sign-in screens.

### Step 7: Download everything afterwards

On a computer, open photos.google.com, find the album, click the three-dot menu and choose **Download all**. You'll get a zip file of everything. Do this within a week or two of the wedding, and keep a copy somewhere that isn't Google.

## Where It Falls Short at a Wedding

Google Photos is built for sharing holiday snaps with family, not for collecting photos from a hundred wedding guests in one night.

### Guests need a Google Account to add photos

Anyone with the link can look at the album. To actually add photos, guests need to be signed in to a Google Account. For many guests that's no problem at all. For an iPhone-using aunt who has never touched Google Photos, it's a sign-in screen at 10pm, and that's usually where she gives up.

### Quality depends on each guest's settings

Photos are uploaded the way each guest's own Google Photos is set up. If someone has chosen to save storage space, their uploads may be compressed. You won't know until you look closely, and by then it's too late to ask for the originals.

### It lives with your everyday photos

The wedding album sits in the same place as your screenshots, receipts and photos of the dog. Not a dealbreaker, just a little less special than a gallery made for the occasion.

### Downloading is a computer job

The **Download all** option is on the website, so plan to grab your photos from a laptop rather than your phone.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a Google Photos shared album free?

Yes. Creating and sharing an album costs nothing. Photos are stored in Google Photos, so keep an eye on your Google storage if you're adding lots of videos yourself.

### Can guests add photos without a Google Account?

They can view the album with the link, but to add photos they need to be signed in to a Google Account. That's the main reason couples with lots of iPhone users or older guests look elsewhere.

### Can I stop people adding photos after the wedding?

Yes. Turn off **Collaborate** in the album's sharing options. Guests can still view the album, but nobody new can add to it. Photos already added stay put unless you remove them.

### How do I download all the photos from a shared album?

Open the album on photos.google.com on a computer, click the three-dot menu and choose **Download all**. Google packages everything into a zip file.

### Can I make a QR code for a Google Photos album?

Yes. Google Photos can show a QR code for a shared album, or you can paste the album's share link into our free [QR code maker](/wedding-photo-qr-code/) and print it on ready-made signs.
