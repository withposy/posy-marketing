On the day

Where to put your wedding photo QR code sign

Put it wherever guests pause with a phone already in their hand. The welcome sign catches everyone on the way in. A card on each table catches them during the speeches, which is when most phones come out anyway. The bar is a quiet winner: people queue, people wait, people scan.

Add a small one to the order of service or the menu, and ask whoever is on the microphone to mention it once during dinner. One mention tends to do more than ten more signs.

Size matters more than people expect. A good rule of thumb is that a code scans comfortably from about ten times its own width:

  • Table card or menu3 to 4 cm
  • Welcome sign on an easel10 cm or more
  • Order of service or sticker2.5 cm minimum
Worth knowing

Static or dynamic: what your printed code really points at

This tool makes a static QR code. Your album link is written into the pattern itself, so the code works for as long as the link does. There is no middleman, no subscription, and nothing that can be switched off later.

A dynamic code works differently. It points at a short link run by the QR company, which then forwards guests to your album. That lets you change the destination after printing, which is genuinely handy. The catch is that the forwarding only works while that company keeps it running, and plenty of free generators quietly stop after a trial, taking every printed sign with them.

Our advice: use a static code, and point it at a link you control and know will not change for at least a year. Then the only thing that can break your signs is you.

A QR code is only as good as the album behind it

The code is the easy bit. What decides how many photos you end up with is what happens after the scan. If guests land on a page that asks them to download an app, sign in, or make an account, a good share of them will quietly put their phone away.
